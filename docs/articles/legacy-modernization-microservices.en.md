# Modernizing legacy systems into microservices

Modernizing legacy systems is a common challenge in organizations that need to evolve without disrupting critical operations. In many cases, the safest solution is not to replace everything at once, but to decompose functionality gradually and strategically.

## Where to start

A well-managed migration usually begins with:

- mapping dependencies and couplings;
- identifying domains with high value for evolution;
- prioritizing services with low ambiguity and high impact;
- reducing risk for critical business rules.

## Decomposition strategy

Instead of dividing the system only by technology, the ideal is to consider the domain and responsibility of each area. This helps create more cohesive services with clearer boundaries.

## Expected benefits

- greater delivery capacity per team;
- simpler and more predictable maintenance;
- the ability to evolve components in isolation;
- better use of modern technologies and infrastructure patterns.

## Risks to control

- increased operational complexity;
- unexpected service dependencies;
- integration failures and distributed consistency issues;
- difficulties with observability and maintenance.

## Recommended practices

- start with well-defined domains;
- use well-versioned APIs;
- maintain automated deployment processes;
- invest in logs, metrics, and tracing;
- validate impact and value gained at each step.

> Modernization is not only about technology; it is also about managing complexity, risk, and business needs.

## Conclusion

Microservices can be a good evolution when they emerge as a response to a real architecture and operations problem. The transition works better when planned, incremental, and supported by observability and governance.
